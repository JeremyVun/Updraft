You are the concept artist for one encounter in Updraft, a real-time Three.js browser game where the player is the
wind. Paint concept keyframes that a 3D build will match, then write `notes.md`. Use your image generation. Save every
image in the current directory with the exact names given, as 1664 x 936 JPG (exact 16:9) unless stated otherwise.

## What the game is
A dreamy, meditative, wordless game: soft golden light, slow time, small kindnesses. A small girl and a grey cygnet (a
baby swan with a bandaged left wing) sail a little wooden boat from island to island; the player never controls them
directly. The player's only verb is moving the cursor: a sweep makes a gust that pushes whatever is under the cursor on
screen, and tracing circles raises an updraft, a rising column. Nothing is ever failed, nothing is timed, there is no
text and no UI.

The story so far: the child was brave for the cygnet in a dark wood; the cygnet was brave for the child on a frozen
island. This encounter is the next step: **together they help someone else**, a whale caught in a net. After it they go
on to mend the stars.

## The reference captures (attached, also in this directory)
`ref-sea-pod.png` and `ref-sea-after.png` are real frames from this chapter, the open sea at sunrise, from the game's
own crossing camera: behind and above the boat, about 24 m back and 6.5 m up, looking ahead. The sun rises low ahead;
the sea is rose and gold; dolphins swim with the boat. **Paint from this camera** (k3 and k4 may come lower and closer
beside the boat, as the game's camera eases in for a held moment, but never a viewpoint the game cannot have). Earlier
concept rounds failed when they were postcards from impossible viewpoints, or showed the puzzle as magic effects instead
of what the player's hand does and how the world physically answers. So:
- Keep the game's rendering style: soft stylised 3D, simple rounded shapes, painterly sea with sun glitter, warm haze.
- Keep the boat exactly as captured: a small rounded wooden dinghy, mast, red sail, a lantern.
- The child: a small girl about 1.1 m tall in an orange hooded coat and a red scarf; the cygnet rides in her brown
  satchel, head and long grey neck out. Nobody ever looks at the camera.
- Show the player's wind the way the game draws it: a few thin soft white wind lines streaking along a stroke, or a
  faint spiral of thin white lines for an updraft. Never a cursor, hand, glowing trail, sparkles or magic glow.
- No text, no UI, no logos, no people other than the child, nothing of a real country.
- Dream logic: impossible things happen calmly. Beautiful first; never horror, never gore, no injury shown.

## The whale
Dream-sized, as a child would dream it: about **75 m long**, far bigger than anything real, so big the frame cannot hold
all of it near the boat. Smooth slate-blue back catching the low gold sun on its rim, paler underside, a few barnacles,
simple rounded stylised forms (not realistic, not cartoon-faced). Its eye, near the boat, is larger than the boat's
hull is wide, with a heavy, tired lid. Its blowhole is on top of the head, a few metres from the eye. It lies still at the
surface, worn out, breathing slowly. An old drifting fishing net is caught over its head and blowhole and trails along
its back: faded brown-green mesh, rows of round cork floats, a few strands of weed. One loop of the net is caught round
its near pectoral flipper. Sad but gentle: the whale is tired, not hurt.

## The sequence, as keyframes
1. `k1-island.jpg` (from the game's normal crossing camera): the pod of dolphins leads the boat off its line toward what
   looks like a long low island in the sunrise haze ahead. Up close it is a whale, lying still, the net and its floats
   draped over its head and back; a weak breath of mist rises and the net over the blowhole bulges with it. The scale
   must astonish: the boat is tiny beside it.
2. `k2-the-breath.jpg`: the boat at rest beside the whale's head. The net lies across the blowhole; each breath only
   domes the mesh and sputters. The player traces circles over the blowhole: a faint spiral of thin white lines stands
   there, and the updraft lifts the mesh up off the blowhole like a sheet in the wind, so the whale draws its first full
   breath (a soft column of mist up through the spiral) and its great eye opens and looks at the child.
3. `k3-the-child.jpg`: a line of cork floats trails from the net across the water near the boat. The player's sweep
   (thin white wind lines) has pushed the nearest float to the boat; the child leans out, has caught the line in both
   mittens and hauls, and the net peels back off the whale's jaw and head into the water.
4. `k4-the-cygnet.jpg`: the last loop is round the near flipper, out of the child's reach. The cygnet has gone into the
   water and swims to it, the loop's end in its bill. The player's sweep along the flipper makes it lift lazily, and the
   loop slides off into the cygnet's pull. The child watches from the gunwale, mittens to her mouth.
5. `k5-free.jpg` (the game's normal camera, or eased back a little): free, the whale spouts a tall golden plume of spray
   against the sunrise, the empty net drifting away on the water; the dolphins leap round it; the cygnet is back in the
   boat, neck up; the child waves. Then (not painted) it rolls, lifts its flukes high as if waving, and sinks, and its
   swell lifts the boat.
6. `k2-portrait.jpg` (936 x 1664, upright phone): the moment of k2, composed for portrait, the blowhole, the eye and the
   boat stacked up the frame.
