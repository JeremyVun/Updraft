# Dandelion clock — crossing keyframes

Three concept targets for the optional birches-to-cloud-stairs crossing. The boat keeps sailing whether the player touches the clock or leaves it alone. There is no completion gate, countdown, camera cut, caption or extra interface.

## Shared framing and layout

Use the existing crossing camera, approximately 24 m behind and 6.5 m above the boat, on the rear quarter opposite the sail. Keep the existing 4.2 m hull, rig, red knitted scarf sail, seated 1.1 m child and satchel-carried cygnet. The pictures borrow the rear view of `ref-crossing-b.png` and the small-boat scale of `ref-crossing.png`.

The hull occupies the lower middle, with its centre near 53% across and 69% down. The horizon stays near the upper quarter. Leave the open water on the visible port side clear for the clock and the rising seeds; keep the gold glitter farther left so the clock has a blue backdrop. The birch shore recedes at screen right. Do not move the camera to magnify the encounter.

Metre values below are proposed blockout targets, not measurements recovered from the generated images. Origin is the boat's centre at mean water level. **x** is starboard, **z** is forward, **y** is height above water. Bearing is measured from the bow: negative is port. Distances are horizontal from the boat centre. Floating heights follow the local swell.

## k2-blown.jpg

Fine white wind strokes touch the source at the water, and the seeds pull free into an airy plume, rising away through the clear port side of the frame. The stripped brown head and its stem remain behind on the surface. The child starts following the seeds with her gaze.

**What reads.** The seed trail begins at the changed head, so the physical cause and result share one view. Individual little parachutes remain legible against the blue sea; the thinning upper plume crosses the cloud into the sky. ## Build risks and limits of these paintings

- **Fine detail at the play camera.** The 0.7 m clock is only about 30–40 pixels across here. Preserve the round silhouette first, then use a restrained seed-card/detail treatment. Thin seeds and wind strokes can vanish or shimmer at lower render scales; inspect real motion before increasing size or brightness.
- **Camera and sail occlusion.** The clock must remain on the visible quarter while the boat passes it. These concepts show it alongside rather than freezing it at the bow. Keep the existing camera; do not solve overlap with a close-up or a camera swing.
- **Contact and attachment.** The white sphere, stripped head, short stem and local waterline need coherent bobbing. Keep a gap from the hull. The girl's satchel follows her torso and the cygnet follows the bag; do not let the lean detach or intersect them.
- **Wind clarity.** Sample the actual sweep at the head and the released seeds. The current pointer projects onto ground or sea (`src/input/pointer.ts`); verify that a stroke over the raised seed head reaches it, using the existing visible-object brushing approach if needed. Keep the thin strokes on that contact, not attached to the child, and avoid turning the seed stream into a continuous glowing ribbon.
- **Star transition.** Fading a nearby particle directly into a stationary point can pop or slide. Transfer only after the seed becomes very small and is above cloud; preserve its apparent direction and cloud occlusion. The final sky scatter should stay faint after the game's post-processing.
- **Lighting and continuity.** The generated sea glitter is stronger and finer than the captures, the green stem catches very warm gold, and the last image drifts the horizon down by about 3% of frame height. Match the game's existing sea and materials, preserve a green stem in shade, and lock the real crossing camera across all three beats. Do not reproduce those generation variations as new engine requirements.
- **Optional play and mobile cost.** Release only seeds affected by wind, allow partial and repeated sweeps, and leave unplayed clocks behind. Bound the number of live seed instances, avoid excessive transparent overdraw, and do not make the star result an objective or completion check.
