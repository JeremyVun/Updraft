## Cursor and movement

The fog's breath gives the empty sails a slow idle movement. Near the hub, a soft stroke draws approximately one and a quarter turns clockwise, with a fading travelling tip showing direction; accept broad, imperfect circles around the hub rather than requiring the cursor to trace the line precisely. Tangential cursor movement supplies torque, and small cloth movements make the response visible before the whole sail gathers speed.

As the boarding crossbar arrives, the rotation eases into a generous dwell and she steps on herself. Continued circling carries her clockwise up the left side and over toward the right-hand roof; at the landing, it eases again while she transfers without a click, release gesture or timing test. Cap the speed so frantic drawing never makes her unsafe.

Wrong-way circling makes an empty mill answer with a small reverse movement, while the invitation quietly redraws the useful direction. Once she is aboard, reverse input produces a soft checked rock rather than sending her back toward the water; resume forward motion from the attained position. When the player stops, the mill coasts a little and settles, she holds comfortably, and the invitation returns after a pause; neither fog nor a missed boarding pass creates a failure or deadline.

## Build judgement and risks

It earns its place if it is a short change of feeling: the tree opens a horizontal route, this mill quietly lifts her above the fog, and the swing later supplies the leap toward the church. Circling a fixed centre is also a clearer wind gesture than another pushable bridge. Cut or shorten it if it becomes a long second pumping exercise before the swing.

Start the blockout with a girl about 1.15 m tall, a roughly 2 m tower diameter, a hub about 2.9 m above water and a 2.4 m sail radius; tune the boarding crossbar near 0.7 m and the landing near 4 m. The full rotor must clear water, wall, roof, satchel and scarf throughout the occupied arc, with no hidden teleport at either transfer. Keep roof geometry behind the rotor plane so the short step across its depth is possible without the sweeping sail cutting through the building.

Horizon clutter is the first risk: no complete four-sail cross, tall conical cap or new landmark tower, and let the mill leave frame for the destination reveal. Scale is the second: a larger wheel would force a larger roof and spoil the sparse village. Holding on is the hardest animation problem—prototype grip changes and planted boots through the whole arc before committing art, and keep the cygnet tucked safely clear of the lattice.

These are generated concept frames, not captures or proof of a working Three.js mechanism; their wet stone, cloth and lighting are somewhat richer than the current game frames. Build toward the actual game's simple materials and meshes rather than importing that extra surface detail.
