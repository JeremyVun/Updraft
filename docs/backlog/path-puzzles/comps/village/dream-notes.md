# Dream village

## The four images

**dream-arrive.png — 1600 × 900.** Broken rows of simple roofs surround an open approach, with small golden silhouettes continuing along the left horizon and cooler clusters on the right. A tall cottage and its tiny neighbour share a washing line, while a single red door shows through the water below the taller gable. The spire and lighthouse sit together beyond the village, with clear sky around them.

**dream-cat.png — 1600 × 900.** The kitten remains the focus above a broad, plain plum roof, with the tub and bow lantern in the foreground. The village continues behind it in several depths, and the tall-and-tiny pair and small washing line sit to the left of the rescue. The near water stays open, and every house window stays dark.

The church spire stands beside the distant lighthouse in a gap between roofs and the bare tree; a submerged red door brings the home motif into the near view. **dream-kit.png — 1600 × 1200.** Six related house shapes share plain roof planes, pale gables, small dark windows and softly bevelled edges. These are modelling targets, not dimensionally exact orthographic drawings.

## Three dream touches

1. **Shifting scale and a slight lean.** The familiar gable repeats with altered proportions, including a tiny cottage immediately beside a tall narrow one. It makes the village feel remembered imperfectly without needing a new decorative object. Keep leans around 5–8 degrees and use them on a few houses only; avoid a whole village of crooked buildings.
2. **One red door beneath the water.** A single quiet patch of the home's red appears on a submerged wall. It earns its place by connecting the drowned village to her destination, without becoming a portal, a glowing objective or a second sail-sized red accent. Build one instance in the room; the paintings demonstrate its treatment from different compositions rather than specifying three separate doors.
3. **Washing between chimneys.** Two small cream sheets, with a narrow faded red hem, recall the earlier washing room. The domestic action continues where it should be impossible, with nobody present to explain it. Use one line in the room, attached to actual chimneys, with only a little movement.

No underwater street lamp, standalone window, chair or extra reflection trick is needed. The near warm light belongs to her lantern; the distant lighthouse remains a restrained navigation light.

## Build order

1. **Village layout and familiar cottage.** Start with the 4 m gabled mass, one plain capped chimney and one dark window. Arrange instances as interrupted rows and small groups on every side, with most density in the middle distance and toward the horizon. Leave the sailing foreground, rescue approach and former village green as open water. Check the whole run before adding detail.
2. **Landmark spacing.** Place the simple spire beside the red-and-white lighthouse, small and far away. Protect their silhouettes and the view toward the fog throughout the camera path. Use atmospheric colour and diminishing size to separate distant layers.
3. **Proportion family.** Add the 2.5 m tall cottage, 1.8 m tiny cottage and 5.5 m wide cottage using the same construction. Add the 4 m hip roof and 6.5 m joined cottages only once the first four give convincing group silhouettes. Vary plum, dusty blue, faded rose and ochre across broad planes rather than adding roof patterns.
4. **Kitten cottage.** Apply the same plain roof material and restrained bevels to its existing geometry. Preserve the exact chimney perch, kitten position, tub position, collision and rescue clearances in the game.
5. **The two unique domestic pieces.** Add the submerged red door and the short washing line. The door can be a simple coloured plane under the existing water treatment; the sheets need only a few broad folds. Neither requires an elaborate asset or new lighting system.
6. **Restraint pass.** Keep the existing sky and water, moderate mesh faceting and soft gradients. Use the kit's plain flat chimney caps as the final authority, removing incidental pegs or pot suggestions from the scene paintings. Reduce contrast and geometry in far roofs instead of accumulating detail.

## What would break the dream

- Too many surreal objects visible at once, or repeating the unique door and washing line throughout the village.
- Comically bent houses, animated antics, eerie openings, black eye-like pairs of windows, ruined buildings or a sense of disaster. These houses are intact and quiet.
- Slate courses, tile geometry, brickwork, moss, clustered chimney pots, signs, utility poles, school bells or identifiable regional architecture. Variety must come from mass, colour, height, scale and placement.
- Bright house windows or extra lamps that compete with her lantern. Keep red sparse and the door subdued beneath the water.
- Roofs filling the near water, a continuous wall of buildings on the horizon, or any cluster blocking the church, lighthouse or fog. Water is the village's streets and its green, and it must remain the route through the scene.
