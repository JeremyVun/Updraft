## Build first

1. **Settlement placement and a terrace module.** Reuse cottages in short aligned rows of three to five, turn adjacent rows to imply lanes, and leave broad gaps between clusters. Add distant groups around every side, including into the sun, then sparse farm outliers; avoid an even scatter or continuous ring.
2. **One shared cottage kit.** Gabled and hipped roofs, an optional dormer or dark attic window, narrow exposed upper-wall variants, bargeboards, ridge caps and two-/three-pot stacks. Use broad slate bands in a shared material; reserve actual tile geometry for a few near silhouettes.
3. **Lane and garden pieces.** A pole and single sagging wire span, wall coping, gatepost and fence-top modules. Place them along coherent submerged boundaries, with ample openings for the boat and run; do not scatter them as decoration.
4. **A few distinct households.** Thatch, a barn with lean-to and one exposed-rafter patch, a pub's wordless fish sign, and two or three sparse orchard trees. Make most buildings ordinary; these are occasional recognisable exceptions.
5. **Optional finishing pieces.** A low school bell-cote well away from the church silhouette, restrained moss patches, one perched gull and a pair of ducks off the route. The existing tub, kitten and lantern already provide the foreground story; additional floating clutter is unnecessary.

## Protect the room

- Keep the near water quiet and open. Concentrate added density in broken middle-distance and horizon groups; no solid building wall, dry islands or canal-like enclosure.
- Preserve the route-plan's gold foot route, red boat drift and their meeting places. The roughly 165 m run still needs readable ridges, wall tops, the door crossing and swing green; decorative walls and wires must not become obstacles.
- North is up on the route plan; fog arrives from the south. Keep that approaching water and fog readable from the run, with open views through settlement gaps rather than a screen of roofs or orchard foliage.
- Preserve clear views toward the paired church and lighthouse. Keep poles, wires, trees and the optional bell-cote away from their silhouettes; do not restore a tower at the old church location. Leave the green as a water clearing, not another block of houses.
- Avoid ornate timber framing, miniature brickwork everywhere, thick moss, lush trees, busy debris, uniformly lit windows or photoreal surfaces. Roof shapes and settlement structure should do more work than texture.
- These are generated village paint-over concepts, not pixel-exact compositing plates. For implementation, retain the original cameras, sky, lighting, water and all girl/boat/tub/kitten assets; incidental rendering drift in the concepts is not a proposed change.
