# Treblo prompts for the meadow score

Two generations that share one tune, key, tempo and sound. Brief and reasoning: "Piano hill and the meadow score" in
[design.md](design.md).

| Piece | Game use |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Sleeping Meadow for piano and winds | First half: landing to the stool. Second half: from the greening through flowers, seeds and listening; its ending returns with the feather and carries the shower, rainbow, boat and crossing. |
| 2. The duet | Four wind calls, four piano answers cut into single notes so the player's tracing plays them, and the finale under the greening. |

Make piece 1 first, upload Sleeping Meadow as its reference, and keep generating until one take is right. Then
generate piece 2 with that take as its reference, so the duet borrows its melody, key and room.

Settings for both: instrumental (no lyrics); the same BPM for both if Treblo offers it (about 64); default prompt
strength, raised a little if strings creep in or the flute and piano play over each other. Use the tags Treblo's list
has and skip the rest.

## Piece 1: Sleeping Meadow for piano and winds

Reference: `A Light Left On - Sleeping Meadow - Treblo.ogg`. Length: about 3 minutes (extend if shorter).

Tags: neoclassical, solo piano, alto flute, woodwinds, pastoral, melancholic, nostalgic, minimal, instrumental

> A remix of the reference for piano and a small group of woodwinds. Keep its melody, its harmony and its two moods, but give the strings' parts to the winds.
>
> First half, about a minute: grey and sad. Solo piano only, F minor, very slow, about 64 BPM. Single notes and open fifths two to three seconds apart, short fragments of the tune, then whole bars of silence. Quiet and hesitant; the melody is never finished.
>
> Second half: the same tune turns warm and nostalgic in A♭ major. The piano plays it with gentle chords, a warm, breathy alto flute takes the melody on its second phrase, and clarinet and bassoon hold soft harmony underneath. One modest swell with a soft French horn in the middle, then it thins back to flute and piano and ends quietly.
>
> Instruments: piano, alto flute, clarinet, bassoon, French horn, nothing else. No strings, choir, pads, drones, harp, bells or percussion. Every phrase breathes, with rests between phrases.

Pick the take where the first minute stays bare and slow, no strings appear at all, the turn to warmth is unmistakable,
and the second half still leaves rests between phrases.

## Piece 2: the duet

Reference: the chosen take of piece 1 (Sleeping Meadow if piece 1 is not settled yet). Length: about 2 minutes.

Tags: neoclassical, alto flute, solo piano, call and response, pastoral, minimal, instrumental

> A wordless conversation between a lone alto flute and a lone piano, on the same tune and in the same key and tempo as the reference: F minor turning to A♭ major, about 64 BPM. They never play at the same time until the end. When the flute plays, the piano is silent; when the piano plays, the flute is silent. A full bar of silence follows every phrase.
>
> Four exchanges, each a little warmer than the last. Flute: a short rising phrase of four notes. Piano: a rising answer of four clear, separate notes. Flute: a falling phrase. Piano: a falling answer. Flute: a higher rising phrase. Piano: a higher rising answer. Flute: a phrase that rises and falls. Piano: an answer that rises and falls, the longest. Then two bars of silence.
>
> Finale: the flute plays the whole melody, its rises and falls joined, and the piano, clarinet, bassoon and a soft French horn join beneath it and swell to the warmest, fullest moment of the piece in A♭ major, then settle to a quiet ending.
>
> Instruments: alto flute, piano, clarinet, bassoon, French horn, nothing else. No strings, choir, pads, drones, harp, bells or percussion.

Pick takes where the flute and piano really are alone in turn with silence between (the cuts go there), each piano
answer sounds like a reply to its call, and the finale has one clear arrival to time the greening to. Calls, answers
and finale may come from different takes as long as they share key and tempo, so a take with one perfect exchange
is still worth keeping.

If the generator will not keep the two apart, fall back to two takes from the same reference and settings, one for
alto flute alone and one for piano alone, each a series of short phrases with silence between, and interleave them
in the edit.
