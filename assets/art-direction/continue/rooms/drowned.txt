ROOM: Drowned village (dusk). A long dusk drift between rooftops: homes the water took. The game's camera travels behind the boat, low among the roofs, never surveying the village from above.
The sail is NOT plain cloth. In the game, from the birches on, the sail is made of the child's long red knitted scarf: a deep crimson to brick-red wool, matte, a little sun-faded, knitted so that soft vertical ribs of knit stitches run down it and fine horizontal knitted courses cross it, with a slightly lumpy, hand-made edge and soft folds; it reads as a big knitted woollen scarf stretched on the mast and boom, never as smooth sailcloth, satin or nylon. Its shape is the shape in the capture.
The little clinker-built wooden boat with its knitted wool sail and a small lit lantern on a white post at the bow sails away from us into the drowned village. Ahead and to the right, the steep roofs of drowned cottages (thatch and dark slate, gable ends, chimney stacks with pots) stand straight out of the calm water at different distances; beyond them the church tower with its pointed spire and weathervane, and on the right of the horizon a tall white lighthouse with a soft pale beam. The bare branching crowns of drowned trees break the water on the left and right. A few fallen orange leaves float on the water. The sky is a soft pale gold and lilac dusk with painterly flat clouds; the low sun is off to the left, laying a warm sheen across the water; the sea is calm violet and rose with long soft reflections of the roofs. No birds. 35 mm lens, camera low, a couple of metres above the water, behind the boat.
Lower band: the calm water nearest the lens deepens smoothly into a mid-dark dusky violet, in focus, with the reflections fading out before it and no leaves, glitter or wake in it; the bottom-right corner plain water.
